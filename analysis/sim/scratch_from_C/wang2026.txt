 a b s t r a c t
This article presents a control strategy for quadrotor unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) to achieve 
autonomous landing on moving unmanned surface vehicle (USV). First, to address the challenge 
of safe landing due to USV deck oscillations induced by wind and waves, the Long Short-Term 
Memory (LSTM) network is employed to predict motion trajectories and identify optimal landing 
window. Next, a modiﬁed practically predeﬁned time stable (PPTS) system is established as the-
oretical basis of subsequent control design. Subsequently, by incorporating neural networks into 
the adaptive command-ﬁltered backstepping control framework, the system’s unknown dynam-
ics terms (UDT) and diﬀerential explosion phenomena are eﬀectively addressed. Stability analysis 
demonstrates that the closed-loop system is practically predeﬁned-time stable (PPTS), with track-
ing errors converging to a small residual set within the predeﬁned time. Finally, simulation results 
verify the eﬀectiveness and superiority of the proposed method.
1.  Introduction
In recent years, cross-domain unmanned systems integrating quadrotor UAVs and USVs have garnered considerable research in-
terest due to their operational eﬃciency in three-dimensional air-maritime environments [1]. Such collaborative systems leverage 
the complementary strengths of both platforms to perform diverse missions, including maritime patrol, reconnaissance, anti-surface 
operations, maritime exploration and coordinated multi-domain operations. Successful autonomous shipboard landing (ASL) is es-
sential for UAV-USV cooperation but poses signiﬁcant challenges when operating within the harsh, variable conditions typical of 
maritime environments [2]. Although quadrotors simplify the landing maneuvers compared to ﬁxed-wing UAVs through their verti-
cal take-oﬀ and landing (VTOL) capability, this advantage does not eliminate the fundamental challenge of landing on an oﬀshore 
shaking platform. The problem’s complexity, therefore, shifts from executing a complex landing trajectory to ensuring safe contact 
at the moment of touchdown [3]. 
The ﬁrst major challenge in the ASL of the quadrotor stems from the oscillatory behaviour of the USV in time-varying maritime 
environments with multi-scale disturbances. Even when the quadrotor meets the six degrees of freedom (6-DOF) of relative position 
and velocity requirements for landing on the USV, signiﬁcant attitude deviations can still lead to collisions between the multirotor’s 
propellers and the USV’s deck [4]. This risk is especially acute on smaller USVs, which are subject to more severe and higher-frequency 
motions than large vessels, making the identiﬁcation of a stable landing window both critical and diﬃcult [5]. To prevent collisions 
between the descending quadrotor and the oscillating USV, a short-term prediction of the deck motion can be employed. The descent

decision-making process is a distinctive and important problem for quadrotor-based ASL systems. Deck motion prediction can utilize 
various methods like Extended Kalman Filters (EKF) [7], Autoregressive (AR) models [6], and neural networks (NNs/LSTM) [8,9]. 
This study applies LSTM networks due to their adaptability across sea conditions, online training capability, and strong scalability

ASL implementation faces another challenge in developing robust quadrotor control systems. Currently, several control approaches 
have shown promising results for landing maneuvers, for instance, internal model based PI control [10], nonlinear model predictive 
control [11], adaptive robust hierarchical control [12,13], visual-based tracking control [14], invariant ellipsoid-based method [15], 
adaptive learning control [16], etc. Nevertheless, the aforementioned control strategies mainly focus on achieving asymptotic conver-
gence or uniformly bounded stability, resulting in state convergence to equilibrium (or its neighborhood) as time approaches inﬁnity. 
The control performance of systems can also be speciﬁed through the framework of prescribed performance control (PPC) [17]. 
PPC methodologies aim to enforce pre-speciﬁed transient and steady-state performance metrics, such as steady-state error bounds, 
by transforming the tracking error and constraining it within a predeﬁned performance funnel [18,19]. To ensure both transient 
performance and steady-state error, in [18], a PPC method is proposed for the ASL’s relative position control. While PPC excels in 
shaping the behavioural envelope of the system response, the explicit upper bound of the settling time is often not the primary design 
objective and may not be strictly guaranteed a priori. Since existing approaches lack explicit convergence time constraints, they prove 
inadequate for applications with critical timing constraints, such as the ASL of a quadrotor, where the mission must be completed 
within a speciﬁc time window dictated by the moving deck’s motion. Developing a ﬁxed-time control strategy with guaranteed upper 
bound of convergence time (UBCT) thus becomes imperative for quadrotor ASL. Recent studies have developed ﬁnite-time control 
solutions for quadrotor ASL. Arif et al. [20] proposed a scheme ensuring tracking error convergence within ﬁnite time during descent. 
Meanwhile, Huang et al. [21,22] investigated ﬁxed-time control for helicopters under disturbances. Notably, the UBCT in such ﬁnite-
time approaches depends heavily on the system’s initial conditions of the control system, while in practice, initial state measurement 
is often diﬃcult [23]. Although the UBCT is less dependent on the initial conditions in ﬁxed-time control, the settling time is usually 
determined by a complex function subject to multiple design parameters, making it hard for engineer to set a required convergence 
time in advance [24].
To tackle the drawbacks of above methods, the predeﬁned-time stability framework was introduced [25], oﬀering a fundamental 
advantage through its user-deﬁned UBCT that depends solely on a single tunable parameter. This characteristic makes it more con-
ducive to application of predeﬁned-time controller in some practical systems, such as robotic manipulators [26], spacecraft system 
[27] and multi-UAV systems [28]. In [29], by combining predeﬁned-time control with sliding mode theory, a controller is developed 
to realize the predeﬁned-time synchronization of complex chaotic systems. Recently, several practical predeﬁned-time stability crite-

Additionally, to perform various missions, the quadrotor must adapt to multiple USV platforms without prior knowledge of their 
exact dynamic characteristics. When UDT exist in the system dynamics, adaptive backstepping control is formulated by incorporation 
fuzzy logic systems (FLSs) [30,31] or NNs [32–34], which supply the estimations of the UDT. In [34], an adaptive predeﬁned-time 
backstepping strategy was constructed for space tethered system, and radial basis function NNs (RBF-NNs) are utilized to approximate 
the uncertain nonlinear term. A recognized limitation of backstepping control is its tendency toward “explosion of complexity” 
arising from recursively diﬀerentiating virtual control signal, while the afore-mentioned reports [30,33,34] did not pay attention to 
this problem. To address this issue, [35] innovatively developed a dynamic surface control method, incorporating ﬁlters into the 
backstepping framework to substantially alleviate computational burdens. Recently, an adaptive command ﬁlter-based predeﬁned-
time control strategy is proposed for time-delay nonlinear systems in [36]. To our knowledge, few studies have investigated the 
integration of predeﬁned-time neural network control with adaptive command-ﬁltered backstepping techniques.
Inspired by these observations, we propose an adaptive RBF-NNs-based predetermined-time control scheme to achieve reliable 
ASL for quadrotor UAV on swaying USV under UDT and external perturbations. The main contributions are summarized as follows:
1) Unlike prior studies [10,12,14–16] that neglected the oscillatory dynamics of USV in harsh marine environments, this work 
explicitly accounts for deck motion eﬀects on ASL. By integrating LSTM-NNs, we establish a safe landing time window instead of 
complex mission planning [13].
2) A modiﬁed PPTS criterion is established, which is an extension in [29] and can improve transient performance compared with 
the PPTS systems in [27] and [30]. Besides, diﬀerent from the ﬁnite/ﬁxed-time control [20–22], the UBCT can be determined by 
adjusting one design parameter.
3) By synthesizing a novel adaptive predeﬁned-time ﬁlter with RBF-NNs, the control design eﬀectively eliminates the “explosion of 
complexity” and compensates for UDT in the quadrotor’s dynamics.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the system dynamics and control objective. Section 3 details 
the deck motion prediction and dynamics reformulation. The controller design and stability analysis are developed in Section 4, while 
Section 5 validates the theoretical results through numerical simulations. Finally, Section 6 concludes the study.

to make a balance between the control burden and convergence region. (ii) For the ASL mission, 𝑇𝑐 of the predeﬁned-time controller 
is a critical parameter. It is selected to be strictly less than the duration of the predicted safe landing window, i.e., 𝑇𝑐 < Δ𝑡. This 
ensures that the tracking errors converge to the residual set, and the landing maneuver is completed, before the expiration of the 
predicted period of deck stability. (iii) In practical engineering, the attitude control loop must exhibit faster convergence speed than 
the position loop. This requirement can be easily satisﬁed by setting ̄𝑇𝑐 <𝑇 𝑐 .

6.  Conclusion
This work proposes a comprehensive framework for quadrotor ASL, integrating a LSTM-based deck motion prediction method 
with a neuroadaptive predeﬁned-time control scheme to ensure reliable landing onto a oscillating USV. The LSTM network is adopted 
ﬁrstly to determine a safe time interval for ASL. Next, a neuroadaptive PPTS strategy for position and attitude tracking control is 
proposed by incorporating RBF-NNs into the command ﬁlter backstepping procedure. Theoretical analysis demonstrates that under 
the developed scheme, the overall closed-loop system is PPTS. The tracking errors converge to a small neighborhood around zero 
within predeﬁned time and all the signals in the closed-loop system are bounded, simultaneously. Finally, simulation results validate 
that the ASL mission of the quadrotor is successfully completed by the developed deck motion predictor and control design.
This article dose have limitations. Existing constraints (i.e., physical input and visibility) and a robustness analysis considering 
LSTM prediction errors are closely related to the security of the ASL, which should be taken seriously and investigated in future work.

Proof.  The proof is established by contradiction. Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that the conclusion is false. Then there exists

time 𝑡1.
At 𝑡 =𝑡1, we have Θ(𝑡1) = 0 . Substituting this into the diﬀerential equation:
̇Θ(𝑡1) = −𝑏1 ⋅ 0 −𝑏2 ⋅ 0𝑠 +𝑏3Ψ(𝑡1) = 𝑏3Ψ(𝑡1)

̇Θ(𝑡1) ≥ 0
This non-negative derivative means that at the moment Θ(𝑡) reaches zero, its instantaneous rate of change is pointing upwards (or at 
least not downwards). This contradicts our initial assumption that Θ(𝑡) becomes negative immediately after 𝑡1. If ̇Θ(𝑡1) ≥ 0, then for
